Methodology

How every number on the index is made, and what it cannot tell you.

Ranges, not single figures

Tuition is a number a university publishes. Rent is not — it is a distribution that moves by neighbourhood, by month, and by whether a listing is real. A single figure with a source link promises a precision the underlying data cannot deliver, so every living cost here is a range carrying its sample size and the date it was checked.

Budget: tuition only

The budget you enter is for tuition. Any university charging more is removed from the results; if none are left, the index says so and shows the lowest tuition available for that degree. Living costs are not part of the budget — they depend on choices you haven't made yet — so they are estimated and shown with each result instead.

What the total contains

Annual total = tuition for your fee status + (rent + food + transit + phone/internet + other) × the number of months you pay rent. Nothing is hidden in a coefficient; every dollar traces to a line in data/cities.json.

Degree strength

The biggest part of the index score. For each university and degree it uses a 0–100 strength figure taken from a published subject ranking (QS by Subject, THE by subject, or Maclean's program rankings), with its source attached. Where no subject figure is on file yet, it falls back to the university's overall position on the list — a much weaker signal, and every result that uses it says so.

Salary and job outlook are shown for the chosen degree but play no part in the ranking: they describe the degree, not the university, so they are identical for every school on the list.

The index score

Four components, each scored from 0 to 1: degree strength, admission fit (a logistic curve over the gap between your average and the published cutoff), cost fit (how comfortably tuition sits inside your tuition budget — anything over it is left out entirely), and preference fit (co-op, city size, and crime if you asked for it). Rent

Each city has one published 1-bedroom average. The studio range is that figure, from 15% below it to 20% above, because rents skew upward. Fifteen cities have their own figure; Waterloo borrows Kitchener's (the adjacent city, and far closer than Ontario's provincial average); Guelph, Sherbrooke and Fredericton fall back to their provincial average and say so on the card.

Shared and private rooms are not in that source. They are derived from the 1-bedroom figure — 55% for your own room in a shared place, 40% for a shared room — and every card that uses them says the number was derived rather than measured. Real listing samples would replace this, with the sample size recorded.

Distance from campus changes transit but not rent, because the source publishes one city-wide average with no campus-distance split. Inventing a discount for living further out would be a guess dressed as data.

Food

Groceries come from a provincial table of single-person monthly costs, so every city in the same province carries the same range — Saskatoon and Regina cannot differ here, and a city that is dearer than its province will be understated. It covers groceries only: restaurants and takeaway are not counted anywhere in the estimate, and the card says so.

Eating out

A full meal out is priced at $20–30 and multiplied by the meals per week you enter (times 4.33 weeks a month). Enter zero and the line disappears rather than showing as $0. The meal price is one national figure applied everywhere, so it understates Vancouver and Toronto and overstates the Prairies and Atlantic Canada — city-level prices would fix that.

Transit and the U-Pass

Many Canadian universities charge a mandatory U-Pass in student fees, so transit is not a separate monthly cost for their students. Where that is confirmed for a school, the transit line reads "covered by the U-Pass in student fees" and drops out of the living-cost total. Where it has not been checked, the city's ordinary fare estimate is used, which may overstate the real cost. Only the University of Saskatchewan is confirmed so far.

One gap worth stating: the tuition figures here are tuition, not mandatory student fees. The U-Pass fee itself, along with other compulsory fees, is not yet modelled on either side.

Crime

Off unless you ask for it. When you do, the city's Crime Severity Index — the Statistics Canada 2024 figures, with violent crime shown alongside — is compared with a national baseline and folded into preference fit. Fifteen of the nineteen cities appear in the published table; the other four (Victoria, Quebec City, Sherbrooke, Fredericton) have no figure, and crime is left out of their score rather than guessed at. Two things worth saying plainly: it is a city-wide figure, so it says very little about the specific neighbourhood you would live in; and it counts reported crime, which is not the same as your risk as a student. Use it to notice a large difference between cities, not a small one.

Co-op

Ticking "co-op is a must" drops any program without co-op and nudges the rest by the university's position on a co-op strength list. That list arrived without a publisher or a methodology, so it is treated as a weak signal: being on it lifts a school a little, being absent from it costs nothing. CEWIL Canada placement figures, or each school's own co-op statistics, would replace it properly.

Expiry

Every figure carries a check-by date {{ expiry }} months after it was verified. Past that it is marked stale rather than quietly presented as current, because the dangerous moment for a tool like this is six months after its author stops maintaining it.

Becoming a doctor

Canada has no MBBS. Doctors earn an MD, usually after completing an undergraduate degree; surgery is a residency after that. Most Canadian medical schools admit few or no international students. The "pre-med pathway" option ranks the undergraduate degrees students commonly take first, and says this at the top of the results.

What this cannot tell you

  • Your actual admission odds — the cutoff is a published minimum, not a prediction.
  • Scholarships, bursaries or assistantships, none of which are modelled.
  • Mandatory student fees — health plans, athletics, the U-Pass itself — which sit outside the tuition figure and are not counted anywhere yet.
  • Whether you will like the place. Cost is one input among many.
